The core problem: 19 screens before purchase is long. Best-in-class quiz-onboarding subscription apps (Noom, Fabulous, Rise) typically run 10–15 screens total before the paywall. ROOK's flow has strong content on every individual screen, but funnel math is brutal with compounding steps — even at a very healthy 95% retention per screen, 19 screens compounds to only ~38% completion. Two screens in particular will retain far worse than 95%: the data-entry screen (Screen 7 — name, photo, platform selection) and the OAuth connection screen (Screen 13 — asking for real account access). Those are the two screens I'd worry about most, not the paywall itself.

That model lands around 9% of onboarding starters begin a trial, and roughly 2% become paying subscribers — measured as a share of everyone who opens the app and sees Screen 1. That's actually not far off healthy industry norms for this category (install-to-paid in the 2–6% range is common for consumer security/identity subscription apps), so the number itself isn't alarming. What should give you pause is where the model bleeds users — 76% of the total loss happens between the quiz screens and the paywall, almost entirely in two screens (data entry and OAuth), not in the paywall itself.

A few things that would move this number meaningfully, in priority order:

1. Fix the threat-count inconsistency before anything else. I flagged this in the audit — "11 potential matches" on the results teaser vs. "1 threat detected" on the paywall two screens later. If that's a copy bug rather than intentional (e.g., free tier only shows 1 of 11), a mismatched number at the exact moment someone's deciding to pay is the kind of thing that silently tanks trial-start rate in a way no A/B test will explain, because users don't report "the numbers didn't match," they just bounce.

2. Shorten the path to the paywall, not the paywall itself. The paywall and pricing screens are already well-built — clear terms, "no payment due now," pre-selected annual plan. The leverage isn't there. It's in collapsing Screens 4–6 (three separate one-question screens) into one or two, and reconsidering whether OAuth connection needs to happen before the first scan at all — could you run the first scan on manual handle entry only, and pitch OAuth later as a "richer scan" upsell once trust is already established? That alone could meaningfully lift the number bleeding at Screen 13.

3. Pre-fill instead of re-ask. Screens 4, 5, and 6 all ask related questions (why they're here, who they are, how many accounts) with no visible connection between them — each one restarts the user's effort budget. Carrying answers forward visibly ("Since you're a Creator with 6+ accounts...") reduces perceived length even without cutting screens.

One honest caveat: any specific percentage here — mine or anyone else's — is a modeled guess without paid-vs-organic traffic mix, actual App Store conversion rate, your specific price-test results, or real completion data per screen. Treat the shape of the funnel (where the losses concentrate) as the useful signal, not the exact 2%. The only way to know the real number is to ship an instrumented version and look at per-screen completion rates — I'd wire up analytics on every single screen transition before investing further in the motion polish, since right now you can't tell whether Screen 7 or Screen 13 is actually the leak I'm predicting it is.
