# Rook — Design & Build a Post-Detection Impersonation Resolution System

You are working on **Rook**, an identity-protection application designed to detect online impersonation and fake accounts using a user's identity, photos, name, usernames, or likeness.

## The Product Problem

Rook currently addresses only approximately half of the impersonation problem:

**Detection.**

Finding an impersonator is valuable, but detection alone does not solve the user's actual problem.

The user's real desired outcome is:

> **The impersonating account is removed as quickly as legitimately possible.**

Today, after Rook detects an impersonator, users can still be left waiting on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, or another platform to review a report and take action.

This creates a major gap:

**Detection → ????? → Platform Removal**

We want Rook to own as much of that middle portion as legitimately possible.

---

# Your Assignment

Design a new major Rook feature/system that helps users move from:

**Potential impersonator detected**

to

**Identity verified → impersonation confirmed → evidence prepared → correct platform process initiated → case followed → escalated when appropriate → account removed/resolved**

as efficiently as possible.

You have **full creative control** over the solution.

Do not assume that our existing ideas are the best implementation.

Approach this as a senior product designer, security engineer, trust & safety specialist, platform-integrations engineer, and startup founder working together.

Research/consider what is technically and operationally possible and design the strongest product Rook can legitimately build.

---

# Primary Objective

We want Rook to become more than an impersonation **detector**.

We want it to become an:

## Impersonation Detection + Resolution Platform

The feature should make a paid Rook subscriber feel:

> "Rook doesn't just tell me someone stole my identity. Rook helps me do something about it and stays on the case until it is resolved."

The system should reduce:

* Time between detection and user action
* Work required from the victim
* Incorrect/incomplete platform reports
* Missing evidence
* Users forgetting to follow up
* Users not knowing what reporting method to use
* Users not knowing whether anything has changed
* Repeated manual checking
* Confusion about escalation options

And, where legitimately possible, reduce:

## Time-to-Takedown

---

# Critical Constraint: Stay Within Platform Rules

This feature MUST operate within the rules, APIs, reporting policies, Terms of Service, automation restrictions, and abuse-prevention requirements of the platforms Rook interacts with.

Do NOT implement or recommend:

* Mass reporting
* Coordinated brigading
* Fake reports
* Bot-generated reports pretending to be different people
* Creating fake complainants
* Circumventing platform rate limits
* Unauthorized scraping
* Manipulating platform moderation systems
* Automated actions prohibited by a platform
* Misrepresenting Rook as the victim
* Fabricating evidence
* Anything designed to artificially trigger platform enforcement

The goal is **not to game moderation systems.**

The goal is to make legitimate impersonation complaints exceptionally complete, accurate, organized, easy to submit, easy to follow, and properly escalated through authorized channels.

# Platform-Specific Intelligence

Do not treat every social platform identically.

The system should understand that different platforms can have different:

* Impersonation policies
* Reporting forms
* Intellectual-property processes
* Identity-verification requirements
* APIs
* trusted reporting programs
* business/creator support paths
* documentation requirements
* appeal/escalation paths
* case/reference numbers
* response processes

Design an architecture where Rook can maintain a **platform-specific resolution playbook**.

When a threat is confirmed, Rook should determine the most appropriate legitimate path available for that particular incident rather than sending every case through a generic workflow.

# Evidence

Explore how Rook can turn information it already possesses into a structured, defensible **Impersonation Evidence Package**.

Potential evidence may include information such as:

* Original/authorized profile
* Suspected impersonating profile
* Profile URLs
* Usernames
* Display names
* Profile photos
* Matching images
* Similarity information
* Bio similarities
* Discovery timestamp
* Screenshots
* Changes observed over time
* Connected scam links where legitimately observable
* User confirmation
* Proof that the protected account belongs to the Rook user
* Other relevant evidence

Determine what should and should not be collected.

Evidence must never be fabricated or altered to make a case appear stronger.

Preserve useful evidence because an impersonator may change their username, profile photo, bio, content, or delete the account after discovery.

# Automation

Find every part of this process that can **legitimately** be automated.

However:

Automation must respect platform policies.

Where Rook cannot legally or technically perform an action automatically, design the next-best experience.

Examples could include:

* Preparing information
* Deep-linking users to the correct official process
* Prefilling information where permitted
* Step-by-step assistance
* Generating documentation
* Tracking deadlines
* Reminding users
* Monitoring public status changes
* Detecting removal
* Preparing follow-up information

Use human confirmation whenever necessary.

Never pretend an action was submitted if Rook cannot verify that it was.

---

# Escalation

A major problem occurs when:

**A legitimate report is submitted → nothing happens.**

Design a compliant escalation system.

Determine what legitimate options could exist when a case remains unresolved.

This could differ depending on:

* Platform
* Severity
* Creator/business status
* Fraud/scam activity
* Trademark/copyright involvement
* Account compromise
* Repeated impersonation
* Previous reports
* Available platform programs

Rook should know the difference between:

**WAIT**

**FOLLOW UP**

**PROVIDE ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE**

**USE ANOTHER APPLICABLE OFFICIAL PROCESS**

**ESCALATE THROUGH AN AUTHORIZED CHANNEL**

and

**NO FURTHER ACTION CURRENTLY AVAILABLE**

Never promise removal because final enforcement decisions belong to the platform.

---

# Recurring Impersonators

Think beyond individual accounts.

If one impersonator disappears and another appears three days later using the same photos, Rook should recognize that this may be part of the same ongoing identity-abuse problem.

Explore concepts such as:

**Incident → Case → Campaign/Cluster**

without assuming those exact names or architecture.

Rook should become smarter as legitimate evidence accumulates.
