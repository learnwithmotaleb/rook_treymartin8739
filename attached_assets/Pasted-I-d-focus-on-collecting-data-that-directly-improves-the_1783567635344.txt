# Questions to avoid during onboarding



Don't ask for:



* Age

* Gender

* Income

* Political views

* Race or ethnicity

* Religion

* Home address

* Phone number (unless needed for the product)



These add friction without improving the core experience.



# My recommendation



I wouldn't show all 20 questions to every user. Instead, build an adaptive onboarding:



### Step 1: Identify the user (2–3 questions)



* Who are you?

* What platform do you care about?

* What's your biggest concern?



### Step 2: Ask 2–3 follow-up questions based on their answers



For example:



* A creator might be asked about follower count and monetization.

* A business owner might be asked about brand reputation.

* Someone who says they've been impersonated might be asked about the impact and how long resolution took.



### Step 3: Begin the scan



This approach gives you rich, actionable data while keeping onboarding fast and relevant.



Over time, the responses can help you answer questions like:



* Which user segments convert best?

* Which acquisition channels bring the highest-value customers?

* Which social platforms generate the most impersonation reports?

* Which problems should drive your product roadmap?

* Which user segments have the highest retention and subscription rates?



That kind of insight is often as valuable as the subscription revenue itself because it guides both product development and marketing.
